\tightsection{Abstract}
HEVC intra coding exposes 35 luma prediction modes, making full
rate--distortion optimization (RDO) expensive.  Yet candidate count alone is
not a hardware objective: an FPGA with $P$ lanes executes
$\lceil K/P\rceil$ batches, while replication changes area, routing, timing,
and power.  We propose \project, an evidence-preserving CHIA loop that joins
pinned HM-16.20 experiments, a history-aware proposal controller, analytical
screening, bit-exact Verilator regression, Pareto updates, GCP execution, and
Vivado implementation.  A frozen Adaptive-$K$ policy reduces held-out RDO
evaluations by \metric{38.21\%} while retaining \metric{97.91\%} of Full-RDO
winners; its aggregate Y-PSNR BD-rate is $-0.026\%$, interpreted as no observed
penalty on this limited corpus.  Physical feedback first exposed a 170-level
combinational baseline and then an 80-level serial winner scan.  Resource
sharing, a depth-3 quant/dequant split, corpus-qualified 31-bit cost, and a
balanced reduction tree yield a routed 16-lane kernel at a derived
\metric{68.120 MHz} and \metric{8.134 M candidates/s}, 2.128$\times$ the P1
baseline proxy.  The design does \emph{not} close 200 MHz, and kernel rate is
not encoder throughput.

\tightsection{1. Problem and Opportunity}
Stock HM ranks all 35 modes by rough mode decision (RMD), keeps a PU-size
dependent shortlist, inserts most-probable modes, and performs expensive RDO.
Prior work shows that content-adaptive pruning can reduce this work
\cite{gwon2019}.  The cross-layer problem is that two policies with equal mean
$K$ may have different hardware cost because latency is quantized by
$\lceil K/P\rceil$.  Conversely, adding lanes may reduce batches but fail
placement or deepen a routing-critical cone.

\project therefore optimizes a contract spanning three evidence classes:
software coding quality, cycle-accurate RTL correctness, and routed physical
results.  Analytical estimates are retained but never relabeled as measured
hardware.

\tightsubsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A composable CHIA graph connecting policy search, matched HM curves,
        RTL checks, cloud workers, persistent history, and backend feedback.
  \item A frozen, independently specified Adaptive-$K$ policy, evaluated
        separately from later hardware-aware policies.
  \item A physically driven RTL evolution from monolithic replication to
        serial shared lanes and balanced winner reduction.
  \item Machine-readable identities, hashes, cache behavior, and Pareto
        history supporting replay and claim auditing.
\end{enumerate}

\tightsection{2. Adaptive-$K$ Algorithm and Contract}
The policy treats a low normalized best rough cost as evidence that one mode
already predicts the block well, so a short expensive-RDO prefix is sufficient;
ambiguous blocks retain more candidates.  For each luma PU it executes:
\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{blue}{cyan}{\begin{minipage}{0.91\linewidth}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Adaptive-threshold.v1}\par
1. For every mode $m=0,\ldots,34$, compute
$C[m]=\mathrm{SATD}[m]+\mathrm{bits}[m]\sqrt{\lambda}$.\par
2. Stable-sort modes by increasing $C$; equal costs retain lower mode ID.\par
3. Compute
$\widehat C_{\min}=\min_m C[m]/\{WH(2^{b}-1)\}$ for PU size $W\!\times\!H$
and luma bit depth $b$.\par
4. Select $K=4$ if $\widehat C_{\min}\le0.04$, $K=16$ if
$0.04<\widehat C_{\min}\le0.20$, and $K=35$ otherwise.\par
5. Run candidate RDO on exactly the first $K$ ranked modes; an exact RD-cost
tie retains the earlier stable rank.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}
Unlike our earlier confidence-gap and relative-SATD candidates, this rule uses
an absolute rough-cost difficulty measure normalized across PU area and sample
range.  A bounded comparison on two deterministic discovery workloads selected
the tuple $(0.04,0.20;4,16,35)$ as the lowest-cycle tested adaptive point below
the predeclared 1\% BD-rate gate.  We then froze its identity and evaluated it
without retuning on three predeclared held-out synthetic families.

The controller deliberately excludes $P$, scheduler state, QP, and activity;
hardware latency follows only afterward as $\lceil K/P\rceil$ batches.  Thus
RTL exploration cannot silently alter coding decisions.  All matched curves
use pinned HM-16.20, Main 8-bit 4:2:0 All-Intra, four frames, and QP
22/27/32/37.  Section 5 reports both discovery and held-out trade-offs.
\end{multicols}

\tightsection{4. RTL Architecture, Checked Against Source}
The final top level latches an event containing $K\le35$ ranked mode
descriptors, QP, Q16.16 $\lambda$, references, and a 4$\times$4 original luma
block.  A ready/valid scheduler launches candidate $bP+l$ to lane $l$ and masks
the partial final batch.  Up to 16 lanes operate concurrently; each lane is
internally serial and accepts another candidate only after retirement.

The lane implements planar, DC, and angular modes 0--34; 4$\times$4 8-bit
prediction/residual; two-pass HEVC integer DST; scalar quantization with RDOQ,
transform skip, sign hiding, and scaling lists disabled; inverse transform and
clipped reconstruction; SSE; and fixed-point RD cost.  Depth 3 separates
quantization from dequantization.  One candidate takes 132 cycles and a batch
retires in 134 cycles without backpressure.

The reduction compares valid pairs lexicographically: lower RD cost wins and
an exact tie selects lower stable rank.  In the P16 revision, a $\log_2P$
balanced tree replaces the P-deep scan.  The 31-bit cost is safe for the
observed maximum 1,312,532,898 only; it is not an arbitrary-input proof.

\tightsubsection{Verification evidence}
Final campaigns executed 121,600 vector/candidate checks.  The depth-3 serial
corpus alone contains 8,960 vectors covering all 35 modes and four QPs; the
tree campaign adds 10,560 P1/P8/P16 evaluations including backpressure.  Stage
values, order, winners, cycle counts, dropped/duplicate checks, and interfaces
all passed with zero failures.
\end{multicols}

The frozen policy responds to content.  It removes 13.95\% and 13.30\% of RDO

minimum-risk Bayesian classifier.  Our relative-SATD baseline instead starts
from all 35 modes and uses threshold counting only, so the published result is
context rather than a direct comparison.

\tightsection{6. Physical Feedback as an Agent Input}
The first P-way baseline replicated a monolithic candidate datapath.  At P1,
the critical path traversed prediction, residual, both forward-transform
passes, quant/dequant, both inverse passes, reconstruction, and distortion:
170 logic levels, including 69 CARRY8 levels.  P2 reached 175 levels, and P4
exceeded LUT/CARRY capacity before placement.

The next proposal serialized work within each lane and inserted state
boundaries between major arithmetic phases.  This raised Fmax and reduced
per-lane area, allowing P16 to route, but a serial 16-way winner scan became an
80-level path.  The final balanced tree reduced that path; the routed critical
path is now 39 levels inside forward-transform accumulation and ends at a
residual-sample RAM input.

\tightsubsection{Measured interpretation}
For a failed 5 ns timing constraint, we report
\begin{equation}
 f_{\mathrm{derived}}=\frac{1}{5\,\mathrm{ns}-\mathrm{WNS}},\qquad
 T_{\mathrm{kernel}}=\frac{P f_{\mathrm{derived}}}{C_{\mathrm{batch}}}.
\end{equation}
This is a post-route comparison proxy.  It is neither timing closure nor
encoder frames/s.  The selected P16 design routes with WNS $=-9.680$ ns, hence
$f_{\mathrm{derived}}=68.120$ MHz and
$T_{\mathrm{kernel}}=8.134$ M candidates/s for 134-cycle batches.

\tightsubsection{Resource budget}
On the KV260 device (117,120 LUTs, 1,248 DSPs), the final design uses 52.26\%
of LUTs and 65.38\% of DSPs.  Against the project's 70\%-of-device integration
budget, this is 74.66\% of the LUT allowance and 93.47\% of the DSP allowance,
leaving 57 DSPs.  P16 is the throughput endpoint of the Pareto frontier, not
the minimum-area or minimum-power point.
\end{multicols}

\tightsection{8. Reproducibility and Deliverables}
Local/GCP parity produced identical bitstream, reconstruction, and trace hashes
for a reference encode.  GCP workers later replayed RTL campaigns with zero
failures.  Post-run Compute Engine queries found no remaining instances,
disks, or reserved addresses.  Actual billed spend and promotional-credit
balance remain unavailable and are not replaced by estimates.

The artifact preserves policy versions, manifests, source and corpus hashes,
raw Vivado reports, machine-readable result schemas, and Pareto history.
Principal deliverables are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item CHIA proposal/evaluation graphs and deterministic policy controller;
  \item pinned HM patches, matched quality curves, and synthetic fixtures;
  \item parameterized SystemVerilog, golden vectors, and Verilator campaigns;
  \item analytical replay, model calibration, routed reports, and parsers.
\end{itemize}

\tightsection{9. Limitations and Next Step}
The coding study uses small deterministic synthetic All-Intra workloads, not
standard natural-video classes.  RTL is limited to 4$\times$4 8-bit luma and
excludes chroma, inter prediction, larger transforms, RDOQ, syntax coding, and
hardware CABAC/context evolution.  Exact HM candidate bit counts are supplied
externally.  The 31-bit cost width is corpus-qualified, not formally bounded
for arbitrary input.

All physical runs are out of context and omit board-level clocks, pins, AXI,
and encoder integration.  Most importantly, the selected implementation has
negative WNS and does not close 200 MHz.  The next loop should pipeline or
restructure forward-transform accumulation, now the measured critical path,
then repeat regression and route before any board-frequency claim.

Within these limits, \project demonstrates the intended agentic co-design
behavior: it preserves a software-quality contract, searches reproducibly,
checks a bit-exact kernel, and uses failed backend evidence to produce a
specific microarchitectural revision rather than merely requesting more
parallelism.
